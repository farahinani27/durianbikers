# Category descriptions — batch 1 (top 10 by product count)

Draft for review. **Nothing here is published yet.**

All 60 categories with products currently have an empty description, so these
pages have neither on-page content nor a meta description. Filling the term
description fixes both at once — `durianbikers-seo.php` builds the meta
description from it.

**How these are written**

- The **first sentence is built to stand alone at ~150 characters**, because
  that is what gets truncated into the Google snippet. Everything after it is
  for the person actually reading the page.
- Rider-to-rider: what the part does, what to check before buying, which brands
  we carry. No keyword stuffing — it reads as filler to customers and Google.
- Only brands confirmed present in that category are named. Nothing is claimed
  about price, stock, warranty or shipping.
- Fitment is stressed where the catalogue is fitment-specific (most R&G lines
  are listed per model and year).

## 1. Crash Protectors — 573 products — `/product-category/crash-protectors/`
Brands present: R&G, SW-Motech

> Crash protectors take the hit before your fairings and engine cases do, turning an expensive low-side into a cheap one. We stock R&G and SW-Motech.
>
> Also called bobbins, frame sliders or crash bungs, these mount to the frame or engine and give the bike a hard point to slide on instead of grinding away bodywork. Most riders fit them before a trackday or their first winter commute, and they pay for themselves the first time the bike goes down in a car park.
>
> Fitment is model and year specific — an R&G kit listed for a Z650 '17- will not bolt to an earlier bike, and some kits need a fairing drilled while "no-cut" versions do not. Check the model and year in the product title before ordering, and if your bike is not listed, message us and we will check.

## 2. Engine Case Covers — 457 products — `/product-category/engine-case-covers/`
Brands present: R&G, SW-Motech

> Engine case covers protect your clutch, alternator and pulse covers from impact damage, and are mandatory for most trackday organisers. R&G and SW-Motech.
>
> A cracked engine case means oil on the track, a red flag and a recovery van. These bolt over the original covers and take the abrasion instead. Most are glass-fibre or high-impact plastic, and sold as single covers or as a two or three piece kit for the whole engine side.
>
> Before ordering, check whether your organiser requires covers that bolt through the original cover bolts or simply bond on — rules differ. Fitment is model specific, and the product title lists the bikes each kit is made for.

## 3. Air Filters — 380 products — `/product-category/air-filters/`
Brands present: BMC

> BMC performance air filters flow more air than a stock paper element and are washable, so one filter lasts the life of most bikes. Full BMC range in stock.
>
> A clogged filter costs you throttle response long before it throws a code. BMC's cotton-gauze elements are a direct drop-in replacement — no airbox modification, no remap needed for a standard bike — and they are cleaned and re-oiled rather than thrown away, which works out cheaper over a few services.
>
> Clean roughly every 10,000 km, sooner if you ride dusty or wet roads. You will need BMC's cleaning detergent and filter oil, both of which we carry. Check the product title for your model — filters are shaped to the airbox and are not interchangeable between bikes.

## 4. Helmets — 366 products — `/product-category/helmets/`
Brands present: MT, NHK, Simpson, Axxis, HJC

> Full face, open face and modular helmets from MT, NHK, Simpson, Axxis and HJC, in sizes XS to XXL, with replacement visors and pads available.
>
> The right helmet is the one that fits your head shape, not the one with the best graphics. Most riders are between two sizes — if the cheek pads press hard but the crown has a gap, you are likely in the wrong shell shape rather than the wrong size. Our range covers round-oval and intermediate-oval fits across the brands we carry.
>
> Worth checking before you buy: whether the model is Pinlock-ready if you ride in rain, whether it takes an intercom if you ride in a group, and whether replacement visors and cheek pads are available — all of ours are. Unsure on sizing, send us your head measurement and we will advise.

## 5. Helmets Accessories — 307 products — `/product-category/helmets-accessories/`
Brands present: Bell, NHK, MT, HJC, Scorpion, Axxis

> Genuine replacement visors, cheek pads, liners, Pinlock inserts and vents for Bell, NHK, MT, HJC, Scorpion and Axxis helmets.
>
> A helmet you otherwise like is rarely worth replacing over a scratched visor or a packed-out liner. Cheek pads are also how you fine-tune fit — dropping from 35 mm to 30 mm pads loosens a helmet that is a size too tight without compromising safety, and swapping up tightens one that has worn in.
>
> Accessories are model specific, not brand specific: an MT visor fits the MT model it was made for and nothing else. Check your helmet's exact model name, usually printed inside the shell or on the chin strap tag, against the product title before ordering.

## 7. Visors & Visor Art — 236 products — `/product-category/visors-and-visor-art/`
Brands present: Bell, MT, NHK, Axxis

> Clear, tinted, iridium and photochromic visors plus Bell Visor Art panels, for Bell, MT, NHK and Axxis helmets.
>
> A fresh visor is the cheapest upgrade to night riding there is — scratches scatter headlight glare far more than most riders realise. Clear for everyday and night use, light or dark tint for daylight, iridium for looks and glare, and photochromic if you want one visor that handles both.
>
> Two things to check. First, the visor must match your exact helmet model, not just the brand. Second, dark tints are for daytime only and are not road legal at night in most places — keep a clear visor with you if you are riding home after dark.

## 8. Fork Protectors — 208 products — `/product-category/fork-protectors/`
Brands present: R&G

> R&G fork protectors shield your fork sliders and seals from stone chips, which is far cheaper than a fork rebuild after a leaking seal.
>
> Fork sliders sit directly behind the front wheel and take everything it throws up. One stone chip in the wrong place scores the slider, the scored slider eats the seal, and you are into a rebuild and an MOT-style failure for oil on the disc. A set of protectors costs a fraction of that.
>
> Fitment is by fork diameter and model, so check the product title against your bike. They fit over the slider and most need the front wheel out for ten minutes — straightforward for home fitting, and we can advise if yours is a trickier one.

## 9. Full Face — 179 products — `/product-category/full-face/`
Brands present: NHK, Simpson, MT, HJC

> Full face helmets from NHK, Simpson, MT and HJC — the most protective style, covering the chin bar where a large share of impacts land.
>
> The chin bar is the argument for full face: it takes the hit that an open face leaves to your jaw. The trade-off is heat and a bit more effort at the petrol pump, which modern ventilation has largely solved — most of our range runs multiple intake and exhaust vents.
>
> Look for a Pinlock-ready visor if you commute through rain, a drop-down sun visor if you ride into low sun, and removable washable interiors, which matter more in our climate than anywhere else. All sizing is listed per model, and cheek pads can be swapped to adjust fit.

## 10. Fender Extenders — 177 products — `/product-category/fender-extenders/`
Brands present: R&G

> R&G fender extenders stop road spray and stones from the front wheel reaching your radiator, engine and exhaust headers.
>
> A blocked or stone-damaged radiator is an expensive, slow problem that starts with debris your short OEM mudguard never caught. The extender bolts onto the back of the existing front mudguard and adds the length the factory left off for styling. It is one of the cheapest preventative parts you can fit.
>
> Fitment is model specific and the title lists the bikes each one suits. Most fit with the supplied adhesive pad and two screws, no drilling and no permanent change to the original mudguard.
